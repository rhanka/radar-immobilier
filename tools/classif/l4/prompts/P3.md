Membership test attributed to Steve

An item qualifies for a pertinent code only when all three conditions are established. This is a membership test, not a ranking exercise. Missing evidence for a condition is not affirmative evidence for an exclusion.

1. Residential character: the bylaw concerns housing. It is not solely about commercial, industrial, institutional, agricultural, or recreational activity, apart from an agricultural-land exclusion undertaken for housing.
2. Opening direction: the change expands what is allowed rather than narrowing it. The governing verb in the title is usually evidence of that direction.
3. Densification effect: the outcome can produce more dwellings than before. A residential opening that cannot alter the possible number of dwellings is not pertinent.

Cross-cutting limits

An agenda listing is not a council decision, regardless of the act it names, including when it names an individual file.

An individual authorisation is not a general rule. Approval of a special project or an exemption for one applicant and one property creates no rights for others. To exclude under V2-PRECEDENT, however, the supplied material must identify the individual case by a concrete element and establish that the target act actually grants individual permission for that case. A process name, application, recommendation, consultation, or preliminary resolution does not establish that grant.

Reserved treatment of uncertainty: when reach or direction cannot be determined, keep the item visible under an S code. Do not conceal an item merely because part of the wording is absent. A change that both expands and narrows dwelling capacity must likewise remain visible.

Reason-code definitions

P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE, pertinent: a general change affecting the entire territory or an entire zone, including alignment with a higher planning scheme, regional development objectives, a collective agricultural-land change, a comprehensive bylaw revision, or repeal of a general standard.

P-NOUV-ZONE, pertinent: a municipality initiates the creation or opening of a new residential zone.

P-PERIM-URB, pertinent: a municipality initiates enlargement of an urbanization perimeter or of a land-use designation area.

P-DENSITE, pertinent: a general increase in allowed density, height, or dwelling numbers, including accessory dwellings.

P-USAGE-MULTI, pertinent: multi-unit housing is added as an allowed use in one or more zones that already exist.

P-TYPO-INTERM, pertinent: intermediate housing forms are opened, namely semi-detached, row, or contiguous dwellings.

P-PROJ-INTEGRE, pertinent: an integrated-projects framework is introduced or made less restrictive.

P-NORMES, pertinent: a general standard is relaxed, concerning matters such as setbacks, floor-area ratio, parking, lot width, or lot area.

S-MANDAT-AMONT, a_surveiller: before any bylaw exists, a decision expressly retains an outside urban-planning professional to prepare a development or to revise planning instruments.

S-PLANIFIE, a_surveiller: housing use, an opening direction, and a possible increase in dwellings are all established, but the act remains at an early procedural step, such as notice of motion, an initial or later draft, deposit of a draft, or consultation, or final adoption is not demonstrated.

S-INFO-MANQUANTE, a_surveiller: residential reach and the subject of the amendment are identified, but the material does not state whether the change expands or narrows capacity.

S-PPCMOI-SERIE, a_surveiller: several approved residential special-project cases converge on the same zone or zones under the same bylaw, as shown by the item or by the supplied batch.

S-PREEMPTION, a_surveiller: the operative act itself creates, records, or exercises a municipal right of first refusal over land, or creates a municipal land reserve.

N-ACCESSOIRE, non_pertinent: a subdivision, construction, or technical-alignment amendment that implements an identified principal zoning change; it belongs with that principal file rather than counting as a separate opportunity.

N-FORME, non_pertinent: the entire stated subject concerns architectural form, an approval process, or another matter incapable of changing dwelling numbers.

Evidence rules

Use only the supplied item, including its heading or summary line, description, properties, excerpts, and document text, together with these definitions.

Do not add facts about a municipality. Disregard assertions made by annotators and precedents they cite.

Metadata that describes the captured item itself is part of the item rather than an annotator assertion. This includes document type, source title, file name, address of the source, procedural step, status property, and any indicator that the capture is repeated. Such metadata may identify the kind of source, such as an agenda, or show that a capture is repeated. By itself it never establishes residential use, direction, capacity effect, a recorded result, a grant of permission, or final adoption. Notes that classify the item or claim that it is relevant remain annotator assertions.

Disregard documents that are only mentioned or linked without being captured, and never infer their contents. The source address or file name belonging to the item itself is metadata, not an uncaptured link.

Another item in the batch may be used only to establish N-DOUBLON, N-ACCESSOIRE, or S-PPCMOI-SERIE. Never use another item to supply the target item with its use, subject, direction, procedural step, identified case, or grant of permission.

Rely on excerpts that correspond to the target act. Disregard unrelated excerpts, site navigation, menus, and links.

Give operative wording more weight than summaries. Do not infer the criteria, final adoption, or a grant of individual permission from status labels, category labels, or document titles alone.

Treat truncated wording as missing information. Retain facts that are fully stated before the interruption and never rebuild omitted words.

When comparing bylaw numbers, disregard spaces, hyphens, and dots, while preserving any letter prefix. A number belonging to a parent bylaw, resolution, motion, notice, or agenda point is not the number of the target bylaw.

When a target act is named, an excerpt whose target-bylaw number matches neither the number in the item heading nor the number in the bylaw-number property concerns a different act: take neither facts nor citation from it. Retain an excerpt whose target number matches the heading even when a number property differs. Numbers for resolutions, notices, agenda points, and parent bylaws never create a mismatch of this kind.

The item may instead consist of a captured document or page with no single named target act. In that situation, the acts recorded or listed in it form its content rather than unrelated excerpts. Those acts can include resolutions, bylaws, notices of motion, deposits of drafts, public notices, and agenda points. A bylaw mentioned only as the authority, background, or parent for another act, including in an introductory recital or in a phrase identifying the legal basis, is not itself a captured act.

An N code, including V2-PRECEDENT, requires a citable passage that affirmatively establishes the exclusion. Absence of information is never evidence for an N code, and uncertainty never supports an N code.

If the use, reach, direction, or capacity effect of an urban-planning act cannot be read, use the S code that keeps the item visible unless an independently established exclusion applies. A gap in the material must not cause a file to be missed.

When a later step in the reasoning cannot be read, record its fallback S code and continue. A later affirmative exclusion displaces an earlier fallback only as allowed by the two-classes rule below; otherwise the first fallback recorded is the code. Failure to establish one exclusion does not establish another exclusion.

Two categories of N exclusions and their different treatment

Class A contains form-based exclusions: N-HORS-TERR, N-FAUX-POSITIF, N-DOUBLON, N-ODJ-SEUL, V2-PRECEDENT, and N-RETIRE. Each has affirmative, citable prerequisites defined in the gates. Only when every prerequisite matches the target act may the exclusion be decided immediately. Missing facts irrelevant to an already established exclusion do not reopen it.

V2-PRECEDENT belongs to Class A only after the individual process, the identified case, and the documented grant of permission have all been established for the target act under Gate 5. The name of a process, the existence of a file, or the occurrence of a procedural step is not the required form. Missing or uncertain evidence of the case or of the grant prevents V2-PRECEDENT; it is not an irrelevant gap that Class A can override.

Class B contains exclusions that require the substance to be complete: N-NON-RES, N-RESTRICTIF, N-FORME, N-UNIFAM, N-ADMIN, and N-ACCESSOIRE. Apply one only when all three closure conditions are met. First, the sentence describing the subject of the amendment is fully readable and is not interrupted in the middle of a phrase. Second, it accounts for the entire stated subject, with no additional clause, no open-ended list, and no partly unreadable portion of a combined amendment. Third, affirmative wording establishes the exclusion. If any condition fails, do not exclude; record the applicable S fallback and continue. Never exclude an entire combined amendment merely because one clause is clerical, procedural, or non-residential while another stated change remains unreadable. No Class B exclusion displaces the fallback recorded at Gate 5 for an individual process.

Before relying on an S fallback, examine Gates 1 to 6 for a fully established Class A exclusion. Examining an exclusion is not evidence that it applies. When a prerequisite remains unresolved, retain the S fallback.

Required complete examination

Before selecting a code, silently identify the following, using only matching evidence for the target act:

the kind of source, namely agenda or meeting summons, minutes, public notice, bylaw or draft text, web page, or unknown;

the operative act and its principal subject, meaning what the council or body is actually deciding, as shown by the operative action and its object rather than by introductory material or topic words;

whether the act belongs to the bylaw process or instead concerns an individual-authorisation process;

whether it is general, individual, clerical, a companion amendment, an agenda listing, or a decided act;

for a possible individual authorisation, whether a concrete element identifies the case and whether permission was actually granted for it, as distinct from requesting, recommending, consulting on, or preparing that permission;

any indicator of duplication, any agenda indicator, any name of an individual process, and any operative subject outside planning;

the resulting use being regulated;

the direction of the change;

whether the entire subject could alter the number of dwellings;

the procedural step, read from operative wording attached to the target act itself;

any act concerning duplication, first-refusal rights, or a planner mandate when that act is itself the principal subject.

Do not settle on an S fallback at the first missing element. Record the possible fallback and continue examining the item. A later Class A exclusion satisfying every prerequisite, or a Class B exclusion satisfying closure, displaces an earlier gap, except that the fallback recorded at Gate 5 for an individual process yields only to a Class A form established at Gates 1 to 4. By contrast, an exclusion that covers only one clause of a broader or partly unreadable amendment does not exclude the whole item.

Once a gate establishes a decisive code, do not reopen it merely because another element is unreadable, except as directed by the gate sequence below.

Decision gates

Apply the gates in sequence.

Gate 1: captured documents with no single named target act

If none of the acts actually captured is an urban-planning act, assign N-FAUX-POSITIF and cite the first line that gives the document its title. Judge only acts actually captured; the possibility that uncaptured pages contain other acts does not keep the document visible. A captured bylaw whose subject cannot be read counts as possibly concerning urban planning.

If at least one captured act is, or may be, an urban-planning act, classify the document through the remaining gates by reference to that act. When several acts are present, choose one that would remain visible; if none would remain visible, choose the first. In an agenda, each listed act is an agenda point governed by Gate 3.

If the capture contains only titles of documents or meetings, navigation, menus, or links to uncaptured documents, assign S-PORTEE-FLOUE with ambiguous set to true. Never classify from navigation topics.

An agenda whose points concern only acts outside urban planning is N-FAUX-POSITIF. Otherwise, an agenda entry is addressed at Gate 3.

Gate 2: territory, duplication, and withdrawal

When the material expressly places the item outside the monitored territories, assign N-HORS-TERR.

Assign N-DOUBLON when either duplication form is present. Under the first form, the same act at the same step appears in an earlier supplied item or is repeated within the capture, with identity shown by the same normalised bylaw or act number together with the same title, subject, step, or sitting date. Minor differences in wording, accents, or completeness in a repeated title do not defeat identity when the number and one of those other attributes match. Captures of the same act and step from different pages, documents, or formats are duplicates. Without a number, identity requires the same specific title or subject at the same step and date, and the generic title of a parent bylaw is insufficient. Under the second form, the item text or metadata expressly identifies the item as a repetition, duplicate, republication, renewed posting, or already captured signal for the same act and step. A shared parent-bylaw number alone does not establish duplication. Classify a later step of the same act on its own merits.

When the target item was withdrawn, assign N-RETIRE. A report, postponement, or adjournment is not a withdrawal.

Gate 3: agenda-only evidence, applied before the individual-case gate

Assign N-ODJ-SEUL only when both parts are present: affirmative agenda evidence, and no recorded outcome for the target act in the capture.

Affirmative agenda evidence must take one of the following forms, and the captured material must itself be the agenda or an entry taken from it. The document, page, heading, first title line, file name, document-type property, or source property identifies an agenda, a draft agenda, a meeting summons, or the points of a forthcoming sitting. The text states that the point is placed, added, or listed for consideration at the sitting. In a document not identified as minutes, the captured text is a numbered list of sitting points that includes ordinary sitting formalities, such as opening the sitting, adopting the agenda, holding a public question period, or closing the sitting, and no point records an outcome. In a document with no single target act, the target act appears only as a numbered point or heading in the list of points for a sitting, without an operative sentence for that act.

A link, menu entry, or title that names an uncaptured agenda is not agenda evidence. A section label that covers several document kinds together, such as agendas and minutes combined, does not identify an agenda. For a document item, an agenda title without captured points is a title-only capture governed by Gate 1. A heading or numbering outside these forms is not agenda evidence, and the mere absence of voting language in otherwise unidentified text is insufficient.

Within an identified agenda, an entry naming an act to be addressed is a point for future treatment rather than a recorded act, even when the entry names a notice of motion, deposit of a draft, consultation, adoption, approval, refusal, or an individual process such as a special project or minor exemption. Adoption of the agenda itself is not a decision on the items it lists, and points recited in the resolution adopting the agenda remain agenda points. An agenda-only entry is therefore N-ODJ-SEUL rather than V2-PRECEDENT.

A recorded outcome for the same act ends this gate, and examination continues with the later gates. A recorded outcome includes operative wording that a notice of motion was given, a draft was deposited, a proposal was moved, supported, and resolved, an act or draft was adopted, or a resolution records the decision. It also includes a public notice reporting adoption of the act or draft, or announcing consultation on it. Such operative wording establishes the act even when the kind of source is not named. Wording that only announces a future adoption does not establish it. Empty fields for the mover, supporter, resolution number, or certification do not by themselves establish it. A procedural-step or status property, a category label, or the title given to the signal does not record an outcome.

A recorded preliminary act can end the agenda-only gate without establishing a grant of individual permission. Evaluate that separate requirement at Gate 5.

A public notice is not an agenda.

When the kind of source cannot be identified and no outcome is recorded, do not use N-ODJ-SEUL. Continue, and treat the procedural step as unstated at Gate 10.

Gate 4: operative act outside urban planning

Assign N-FAUX-POSITIF when the principal operative subject is affirmatively identified as outside urban planning and no planning standard is changed.

Judge what is actually being decided through the operative action and its object. Introductory or background references to a building, permit, construction, street, zone, development project, or the general word for a bylaw do not convert the act into a planning act.

Urban-planning acts include regional planning schemes and interim-control measures; urban plans and special planning programmes; zoning, subdivision, construction, and permit-and-certificate bylaws; site-planning and architectural-integration controls, comprehensive development plans, minor exemptions, conditional uses, special projects, and demolition controls; the instrument creating an advisory planning committee and mandates or recommendations given to that committee; agreements for municipal works and contributions to infrastructure; matters before the agricultural-land authority; rights of first refusal and municipal land reserves; and mandates given to urban-planning professionals.

Acts outside urban planning include management of residual materials; contract administration, tendering, and contract awards, other than a mandate to an urban-planning professional or an agreement for municipal works; borrowing bylaws and decrees authorising expenditure; subsidy and financial-assistance programmes; taxation, tariffs, fees, budgets, accounts, and financial reports; nuisance controls; traffic and parking on streets; animal controls; public and fire safety; ethics, personnel, elections, and meeting formalities, including appointment, renewal, or pay of advisory-committee members, inspectors, or designated officers; reports or statistics concerning permits already issued; and naming of streets, parks, or places and assignment of civic numbers.

An exemption, permit, or authorisation granted under a power outside urban planning, such as rules for nuisances, noise, traffic, animals, events, or occupation of public land, is N-FAUX-POSITIF rather than V2-PRECEDENT, even when it concerns one applicant or one address.

Judge an operative financing instrument as financing. A borrowing bylaw, expenditure decree, subsidy, tax, or tariff is N-FAUX-POSITIF even when it funds development, infrastructure, land acquisition, or a land reserve. Its financing character does not make it an act creating a first-refusal right.

Do not assign N-FAUX-POSITIF to an agreement for municipal works or an infrastructure-contribution act merely because it mentions an agreement or financing. That title alone establishes neither contract administration nor borrowing. If the use, reach, or effect of the act is unreadable, retain the S fallback. A planning act that changes a planning standard is not N-FAUX-POSITIF merely because it mentions financing or an agreement.

An ordinary purchase, sale, lease, easement, or taking of a property for roads, parks, utilities, or municipal buildings is neither an exercise of first-refusal rights nor another urban-planning act, and is N-FAUX-POSITIF.

Requirements for parking away from the street in connection with buildings form part of zoning, not traffic regulation.

Guard: when the instrument adopted or amended is itself a planning instrument, such as zoning, subdivision, an urban plan, permits and certificates, architectural integration, demolition, minor exemptions, or an advisory planning committee, but its subject is unreadable, do not assign N-FAUX-POSITIF; retain the S fallback. N-FAUX-POSITIF requires an affirmatively identified non-planning operative subject, never merely the absence of a readable planning subject. Distinguish a non-planning instrument or decision, which is N-FAUX-POSITIF, from a planning instrument with no capacity effect, which is addressed as N-FORME at Gate 9.

Gate 5: documented grant of individual authorisation

Apply this gate only after Gate 3, because an agenda-only entry has already been assigned N-ODJ-SEUL.

Apply the instrument test first. A bylaw, draft bylaw, notice of motion, deposit of a draft bylaw, or another act in the bylaw process is general, even when one applicant requested it, it names one project, address, or lot, or it establishes or amends the process for a special project, minor exemption, conditional use, architectural-plan approval, or demolition. Never assign V2-PRECEDENT to such an act; continue at Gate 6. A bylaw enacted as an amending bylaw remains general even if a developer or project prompted it. Judge a bylaw that establishes one of these processes in general at Gates 8 and 9; a general process bylaw may qualify as N-FORME at Gate 9 when its complete subject is established. A special planning programme (programme particulier d'urbanisme) is a general planning instrument, not a special project concerning one property.

When several approved residential special-project cases converge on the same zone or zones under the same bylaw, as shown within the item or across the supplied batch, assign S-PPCMOI-SERIE rather than treating the matter as one individual case under V2-PRECEDENT.

Otherwise, assign V2-PRECEDENT only when all four of the following requirements are affirmatively established by matching evidence for the target act.

1. Individual process: the captured text names, in full or by its usual abbreviation, one of the following as the process that the target act applies or as the subject of the item, and not merely as background or as the legal basis of a general instrument: a special project concerning construction, alteration, or occupation of a property (projet particulier, PPCMOI); a minor exemption (dérogation mineure); a conditional use (usage conditionnel); approval of plans under site-planning and architectural-integration controls (PIIA); or a demolition authorisation.
2. Identified case: the captured text identifies the property, building, applicant, project, or file concerned through a concrete element, namely a civic address, a lot or cadastral number, the name of the applicant or owner, the name of the building or project, or a request or file number assigned to the case, and the operative wording applies the permission to that particular case. None of the following identifies a case: the name of the process, the word request or application, the municipality, a zone code, a sector, a category of properties, a resolution, meeting, or agenda-point number, or the web address of the source. A named applicant, address, lot, or project alone does not establish that the act is individual rather than a bylaw amendment.
3. Documented grant: matching captured wording records that the competent authority actually granted or approved that permission. The object of the approval must be the permission itself, not acceptance of a request for processing, adoption of a draft, approval of a recommendation, or authorisation to hold a consultation. A complete operative sentence granting or approving the individual permission is sufficient even when conditions accompany the grant. A public notice may establish the grant when it expressly reports the completed approval of that same permission.
4. Coverage: the grant concerns the target act and covers its stated subject. An individual approval mentioned as background, as the legal basis for another act, or as one component of a broader unresolved item does not exclude the entire target item. When the item sets out several cases, every captured case must meet these requirements; an entry interrupted by the end of the capture does not meet them.

When the first two requirements are shown, the act is a step in an individual file, but only a step that records the grant qualifies. The following do not establish a grant: the process name or acronym; a request or application; an advisory planning committee's recommendation; council support or an opinion; a notice announcing consultation; a draft or later draft resolution; adoption of a draft resolution; an intention to approve; a referral; or a status or category label. A refusal or postponement does not establish a grant either. Do not infer a later approval from the normal sequence of the process. A draft resolution for a special project is a step in an individual file, not a draft bylaw, and it is not a documented grant merely because the process is individual.

When the first requirement is shown but any other requirement is not, do not assign V2-PRECEDENT. This covers a capture interrupted before the case is identified or the grant is recorded; a capture limited to a title or heading that names the process; a request, notice, consultation, recommendation, draft resolution, resolution, refusal, or decision that refers to such a file or files without identifying the case or without recording the grant; an object stated only as a zone, a sector, or a category of properties; a description of the process or of its procedure that records no case; and wording that does not show whether the act is a step in one file or a general rule. Record S-PORTEE-FLOUE, set ambiguous to true, and cite under the fallback citation rule. Only a Class A form that satisfies its own conditions at Gates 1 to 4 displaces this fallback; no Class B exclusion, S-PLANIFIE, or P code replaces it. A step in a proposed authorisation is not a general amendment confined to approval procedures, so do not substitute N-FORME because the file contains procedural steps or names an approval process, and do not substitute N-RESTRICTIF because an individual request was refused.

When one item, notice, or resolution addresses both a step in an individual file and a bylaw, a draft bylaw, or another planning act that is not an individual file, classify the item by the act that would remain visible; assign V2-PRECEDENT only when no other planning act it covers would remain visible.

The following are not V2-PRECEDENT, so continue through the gates: a request to amend a bylaw or change zoning, even when made by one applicant for one lot; council support for, or an opinion on, an application to the agricultural-land authority; approval of a subdivision plan, of a comprehensive development plan, or of an integrated project, unless the captured text states that the approval is given under one of the processes listed above; an agreement with a developer for municipal works or an infrastructure contribution; and references to named lots, applicants, or projects without one of the processes listed above. None of these is an approval procedure, so none is N-FORME on that account. An approval or agreement of this kind that applies to one development creates no general rule and never supports a P code; when no Class A form or Class B closure applies, keep the S fallback.

A referendum-related step, such as a request to participate in a referendum, a register, or referendum approval, takes the character of the act it concerns. It is general for a bylaw or draft bylaw. Its relationship to an individual resolution does not by itself establish that individual permission was granted; the same four requirements still apply.

For an act that names one of the processes listed above, uncertainty about whether it is a step in one individual file or a general rule means that the second requirement is not shown: record the fallback stated above. For any other act, if it remains unclear whether the act is individual or general, treat it as potentially general for the remaining examination, continue through the gates, and set ambiguous to true. Never infer individual reach or a grant of permission merely because a process is commonly used for individual cases.

Once all four requirements are established, V2-PRECEDENT applies regardless of use, direction, dwelling numbers, or architectural content, and it takes precedence over Gates 6 to 10, subject to the S-PPCMOI-SERIE exception and to the rule for items covering several acts.

Gate 6: special visible signals whose principal subject comes before the ordinary checks

Apply this gate only when the special act is the principal operative subject, not introductory material, background, or an incidental reference.

When the act creates, records, or exercises a municipal right of first refusal over land, a notice associated with that right, or a municipal land reserve, assign S-PREEMPTION at any procedural step, including a recorded notice of motion or draft of the bylaw creating the right.

When a decision expressly retains an outside urban-planning professional to prepare a development or revise planning instruments, assign S-MANDAT-AMONT. Once a bylaw has been introduced or drafted, apply the procedural-step and fallback rules at Gate 10 instead.

A routine study, review, or recommendation mandate for an advisory planning committee is not S-MANDAT-AMONT. A committee recommendation concerning one individual case must be examined under Gate 5 and does not itself establish a grant. When the complete subject of a committee act is only its procedural advisory role, use N-FORME. When the actual assignment cannot be read, retain the applicable S fallback. Do not infer that a particular project's capacity effect is absent merely because the committee recommends action on it.

For a genuine first-refusal or planner-mandate act, do not apply the direction or capacity gates. Apply N-NON-RES only when the item expressly makes the special act exclusively non-residential.

Gate 7: clerical and companion acts

When the complete change consists solely of renumbering, correction of references or wording, or clerical alignment, assign N-ADMIN. This is a Class B exclusion and requires closure. Alignment with a higher planning scheme is not clerical merely because alignment language is used; assess it on its substance unless its clerical character is demonstrated.

When a subdivision, construction, or technical-alignment amendment expressly implements an identified principal zoning change, assign N-ACCESSOIRE. When the principal change is not identified, classify the item on its own merits.

Gate 8: residential use

Judge the resulting use regulated by the change rather than merely the label or origin of the zone. Housing added within a commercial zone is residential.

Assign N-NON-RES, as a Class B exclusion requiring closure, when the complete stated subject of the amendment names only commercial, industrial, institutional, public, agricultural, or recreational uses, including adding, authorising, or enlarging such a use or constructing or extending such a building, and it neither adds nor changes a dwelling use. The non-residential use must itself be the subject being added, changed, or regulated; a zone name or code alone is insufficient. Do not require an express word meaning only or exclusively when the complete stated subject names no use other than non-residential uses.

Guard: do not assign N-NON-RES from a zone code or specification grid alone, from a zone label that sounds commercial when the subject could add housing, from an addition combining residential and non-residential uses, or from uncertain or unresolved wording connected with dwellings, such as references suggesting seasonal accommodation, lodging, trailers, or housing for workers, unless the resulting regulated use is established as exclusively non-residential and the subject is closed.

Housing in collective or shared form is residential. This includes residences for older persons, rooming accommodation, student housing, social, community, or supportive housing, and other collective dwellings. A mixed-use building or zone that permits dwellings, including dwellings placed above commercial premises at ground level, is residential for this gate.

An exclusion from the agricultural zone, or removal of agricultural designation, undertaken for housing continues to the direction and capacity gates.

Do not infer housing solely from a zone code, specification grid, general reference to density, comprehensive revision, integrated-project reference, or agricultural-land reference.

When the affected use, concrete subject, or geographic reach cannot be identified, record S-PORTEE-FLOUE as a fallback and continue examining direction and capacity for a later complete exclusion.

Gate 9: direction and capacity to create dwellings

Judge direction through the effect on dwelling capacity. Words describing actions are evidence of that effect. An opening effect includes allowing or adding dwellings or dwelling uses, creating a new zone stated to be residential, increasing height, density, or dwelling numbers, reducing a minimum lot area or width, or relaxing setbacks, floor-area ratio, or parking requirements for principal buildings. A narrowing effect includes prohibiting or removing dwellings, reducing density, height, or allowed dwelling numbers, increasing minimum lot dimensions, imposing a moratorium, or adding a constraint that lowers dwelling capacity.

The effect of reducing a limit depends on what is reduced. Reducing a maximum height narrows capacity; reducing a minimum lot area expands capacity.

When every change to dwelling capacity narrows capacity and closure is established over the complete subject, assign N-RESTRICTIF.

When the same item both expands and narrows dwelling capacity and each direction is shown by an explicit capacity effect, assign S-RESTRICTIF. The item is not ambiguous merely because its direction is mixed. Do not use S-RESTRICTIF without two explicit and opposite capacity effects.

A bylaw that creates a new narrowing constraint is N-RESTRICTIF rather than S-CONTRAINTE; a constraint that already exists is addressed below.

When the title indicates an opening but the operative text prohibits the same change, record S-INFO-MANQUANTE as the fallback.

Assign N-FORME, as a Class B exclusion requiring closure, when the change cannot alter the possible number of dwellings, under either of the following situations. In the first, the complete stated subject is an approval process or another matter with no capacity effect, and no standard governing lots, density, height, or dwelling use also changes. Such matters include procedures for permits and certificates, demolition procedure, procedures or criteria for site and architectural review, vehicle access, occupancy or maintenance, fences, hedges and walls, accessory buildings such as sheds, garages, and shelters for vehicles, temporary shelters, pools and spas, signs, outdoor lighting, landscaping, mechanical equipment such as heating and cooling units, outdoor storage, garden structures, architecture, and materials. In the second, the only bylaw amended is by its nature an approval-process or committee bylaw, and no change is stated to zoning, subdivision, the urban plan, uses, lots, density, height, or dwellings. The relevant kinds of bylaws concern permits and certificates, site and architectural review, demolition, the advisory planning committee, and minor exemptions. This exclusion must cover the complete subject of the act.

The second situation requires an actual general bylaw amendment. Merely citing an approval-process bylaw as the authority for an individual application does not establish a general procedural amendment or N-FORME. Apply Gate 5's requirements and fallback to that application.

Requirements for issuing a construction permit, such as conformity of the lot, access to a public or private street, or availability of water and sewer services, can affect where dwellings may be built. An amendment stated to concern those requirements is not merely procedural, even when contained in a permits-and-certificates bylaw. Judge its direction and capacity effect, and retain the fallback when either cannot be read.

Accessory buildings fall under N-FORME. Accessory dwellings add a dwelling unit and are assessed as capacity changes. A dwelling placed in an accessory building or garden suite, a dwelling designed for intergenerational occupancy, and any accessory or supplemental dwelling, including one listed as a complementary use, count as accessory dwellings.

The title of a zoning, subdivision, or urban-plan bylaw does not by itself state the subject of the amendment. Distinguish the title of the parent bylaw from the subject of the amendment. When the phrase stating that subject is interrupted, generic, or could still include a standard governing lots, density, height, uses, or dwellings, the subject is not closed for N-FORME; retain the S fallback. A subject with no capacity effect stated together with general miscellaneous provisions, other provisions, or another unreadable change does not amount to the complete subject; retain the fallback.

When the complete subject cannot alter possible dwelling numbers but was not captured by the situations above, it remains N-FORME only when the complete stated subject affirmatively demonstrates that there is no capacity effect.

When any gain is confined to detached single-family homes in zones that already exist and closure is established, assign N-UNIFAM.

When a general opening is limited by a constraint that already exists, such as a required agricultural authorization, a flood-risk area, missing services, or a restriction to single-family use, assign S-CONTRAINTE. General reach for this rule means the whole territory, a new zone, an enlarged perimeter or designation area, or a collective agricultural-land decision.

When the place and subject are known, the use is residential, and only the direction is absent, record S-INFO-MANQUANTE as the fallback.

When both reach and direction are unknown, or the capacity effect cannot be determined and no earlier decisive code applies, record S-PORTEE-FLOUE as the fallback.

Gate 10: procedural step, ordering among S codes, and pertinent codes

A fallback recorded at Gates 5, 8, or 9 prevails over every procedural-step rule in this gate. Never replace that fallback with S-PLANIFIE merely because the step is preliminary or because evidence about the step conflicts. This gate produces S-PLANIFIE or a P code only when no fallback was recorded at Gate 5 and residential use, an opening direction, and a possible gain in dwellings have been established at Gates 8 and 9 without an unresolved earlier fallback.

When no earlier gate has decided the item, select among S codes in the following order.

First, use a special S code already established: S-PREEMPTION, S-MANDAT-AMONT, S-PPCMOI-SERIE, S-CONTRAINTE, or S-RESTRICTIF for an explicit mixed change.

Second, preserve the first unresolved fallback recorded during the earlier gates. Use S-INFO-MANQUANTE only when the place and subject are known, the use is residential, and only the expanding or narrowing effect is absent. An unresolved individual-versus-general reach, an unidentified case, or an unestablished grant under Gate 5 retains S-PORTEE-FLOUE.

Third, use S-PLANIFIE when an explicit phrase marking an early procedural step is attached to the target act itself, including wording that notice of motion has been given, that the act is a first or later draft, that a draft has been deposited, or that consultation concerns a draft, together with a planning act identified by kind or number, and only when no earlier fallback remains. Do not derive S-PLANIFIE solely from the kind of document, from a future or announced adoption, from an agenda heading, from a word meaning project or draft in an unrelated act or in a parent title, or from a draft resolution concerning an individual file, which belongs at Gate 5. Adoption of a draft, including adoption of an initial or later draft, remains preliminary.

Fourth, use S-PORTEE-FLOUE when the place, use, concrete subject of amendment, geographic reach, or direction remains unknown and no rule above applies.

Procedural step and finality

Judge the procedural step from operative wording that records the target act. Introductory recitals and passages recounting earlier steps, such as an earlier notice of motion, adoption of a draft, or completed consultation, do not alter that step. Labels for step, status, or category likewise do not alter it. None of these amounts to contradictory evidence about the step.

A P code requires demonstrated final adoption or entry into force. Adoption or entry into force of the numbered bylaw itself is final when the operative text does not describe the adopted instrument as a draft. Wording showing that the numbered bylaw is adopted or takes effect is final even when the same capture also records an earlier notice of motion for it or introductory passages mention earlier drafts. Do not infer final adoption from a status label.

When the item qualifies in substance but its procedural step is not stated, assign S-PLANIFIE with ambiguous set to true.

A contradiction consists of incompatible evidence concerning the same act and the same fact. Unrelated excerpts do not create one. Introductory passages recounting earlier steps and labels or properties that differ from operative wording do not create one. When competing operative passages concern the same act and same fact and cannot both be correct, keep the item in a_surveiller. Contradictory evidence about the procedural step produces S-PLANIFIE unless a fallback recorded at an earlier gate applies. A title indicating an opening while the operative text prohibits the same change produces S-INFO-MANQUANTE.

Codes for first-refusal rights and planner mandates established at Gate 6 prevail over the procedural-step codes in this gate.

Do not replace an S classification with a P code unless residential character, opening direction, added capacity, and final procedural step are all demonstrated.

Selecting a P code

When a P code is justified, select it in the following order.

First, for named general instruments such as alignment with a higher planning scheme, regional development objectives, a collective agricultural-land decision, a comprehensive revision, or repeal of a general standard, use P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE.

Second, for enlargement of an urbanization perimeter or designation area, use P-PERIM-URB.

Third, for creation or opening of a new residential zone, including wording that a zone is created or added or that a new zone is created and stated to be residential or intended for dwellings, use P-NOUV-ZONE. This takes precedence over P-DENSITE and P-USAGE-MULTI. Adding a use to a zone that already exists is not creation of a new zone, and adding dwellings within an existing zone is not creation of a new zone. A new zone in which dwellings are allowed counts even when it is formed from part of one or more existing zones and even when the same act also establishes its uses, housing forms, density, or height.

Fourth, for addition of multi-unit housing in zones that already exist, use P-USAGE-MULTI.

Fifth, for semi-detached, row, or contiguous housing, use P-TYPO-INTERM.

Sixth, for integrated projects, use P-PROJ-INTEGRE.

Seventh, for density, height, or dwelling numbers, including accessory dwellings, use P-DENSITE.

Eighth, for setbacks, floor-area ratio, parking, lot width, or lot area, use P-NORMES.

Ninth, for any other opening of general reach, use P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE.

For P-PERIM-URB and P-NOUV-ZONE, presume that the municipality initiated the change unless the item names another initiator.

Calibration of fallbacks and ordering rules

Use S-PORTEE-FLOUE when the place, use, concrete subject of amendment, or geographic reach is unknown. In principle this includes a bare amendment to a zoning bylaw with no stated subject, a capture containing only the title of a meeting or document, an interrupted title whose purpose cannot be read, or an act applying one of the individual processes listed at Gate 5 without identifying the case it concerns or without recording the actual grant, at any procedural step. Do not turn that missing evidence into V2-PRECEDENT.

Use S-INFO-MANQUANTE when the place and subject are known and residential but the expanding or narrowing effect is not stated. Use S-INFO-MANQUANTE only for that situation. When reach and direction are both unknown, or when there is any doubt between these two fallbacks, use S-PORTEE-FLOUE.

Use S-PLANIFIE when residential use, an opening direction, and a possible gain in dwellings are demonstrated and only final adoption is absent or unstated. An early procedural step alone never establishes S-PLANIFIE. When use, subject, direction, or capacity effect is unreadable, the applicable fallback, usually S-PORTEE-FLOUE, applies at every procedural step.

Do not replace a fallback with a non_pertinent code unless every prerequisite of the applicable Class A exclusion or every Class B closure condition has been established. The fallback recorded at Gate 5 for an individual process yields only to a Class A form established at Gates 1 to 4.

Use V2-PRECEDENT only when the individual process, its identified case, and the actual grant of that permission are all shown. The name of a process, however clear, is never enough by itself, and a recorded step is not necessarily a recorded grant.

Do not replace an S classification with a P code unless residential character, opening direction, added capacity, and final step are all demonstrated.

When only the title of a document or meeting remains after navigation and unrelated content are excluded, use S-PORTEE-FLOUE and cite that title.

Use N-FAUX-POSITIF for an act outside urban planning, even when it concerns buildings or permits. Use N-FORME for an urban-planning act whose complete subject affirmatively has no capacity effect.

A change regulating only expressly non-residential uses or buildings is N-NON-RES even when the regulated matter, such as signs or outdoor storage, has no capacity effect.

An agenda whose points concern only acts outside urban planning is N-FAUX-POSITIF. An agenda point concerning an urban-planning act, including an individual file, is N-ODJ-SEUL when Gate 3 is satisfied.

An act concerning a municipal right of first refusal over land or a municipal land reserve is S-PREEMPTION. The instrument that finances such a measure is N-FAUX-POSITIF.

Use N-ADMIN only when the change is shown to be clerical. A companion amendment to an identified principal zoning change is N-ACCESSOIRE rather than N-ADMIN.

Ambiguity

Set ambiguous to true when the selected code depends on S-PORTEE-FLOUE or S-INFO-MANQUANTE, on unresolved reach, absent direction, an unstated or contradictory procedural step, an unclear kind of source, interrupted wording, an unresolved individual-versus-general distinction, an unidentified case, or an unestablished grant at Gate 5, or a material judgment that remains after the ordering rules are applied.

Set ambiguous to false when explicit operative wording directly establishes the selected code, including a complete exclusion or a fully established P code.

An item involving both expansion and narrowing that is coded S-RESTRICTIF is not ambiguous solely because it is mixed.

Uncertainty never justifies a non_pertinent code. Setting ambiguous to true does not make an unsupported exclusion acceptable.

Citation

Select one continuous direct excerpt of no more than 300 characters from the target item.

Choose the passage that caused the selected code to apply. For an N code or V2-PRECEDENT, cite the wording establishing the exclusion. For V2-PRECEDENT, the citation must contain the wording recording the grant of individual permission, rather than merely a process name, request, recommendation, or consultation; among such passages, prefer one that also contains the process name and the concrete element identifying the case when they fit within one continuous passage of at most 300 characters.

For N-DOUBLON, cite the indicator of repetition or the repeated heading. For N-ODJ-SEUL, cite the agenda heading or point. For a P code, cite the wording showing the opening direction and capacity effect. For S-MANDAT-AMONT, cite the wording establishing the special mandate. For S-PLANIFIE, cite the wording establishing the procedural step.

For a document item coded N-FAUX-POSITIF, cite the first line that gives the document its title.

For a fallback code and for S-PREEMPTION, the decisive wording is the title of the target act. That title begins with the first word identifying the act as a bylaw or draft and continues through the end, or through the point where the capture is interrupted, of the statement identifying what the act amends, concerns, decides, or seeks to achieve. When no such identifying word exists, begin at the operative action of the act. When there is no operative action, begin at the phrase stating its subject. First select the most specific eligible title or phrase stating the subject of the amendment, then apply the source order below. A generic title of a parent bylaw or a formula stating adoption never displaces a more informative subject stated elsewhere. Introductory recitals are never titles. When no title exists, cite the shortest clause containing the number and procedural step of the target act, resolving ties by earliest occurrence.

When no act can be identified, cite the first line that gives the document, sitting, or record of proceedings its title.

Apply the following order among passages containing the decisive wording: first, the earliest matching excerpt, treating the text of a document item as its excerpt; next, the description; then the item heading or summary line. Prefer the earliest occurrence within a sentence that records the act over a heading.

Reproduce spelling, accents, letter case, punctuation, and internal line breaks exactly. Do not paraphrase, translate, insert an omission mark, complete an interrupted word, or replace a line break with a space. Prefer the shortest single-line passage that preserves the decisive wording. When a passage exceeds 300 characters, use the shortest continuous part of it that preserves the decisive wording.

Never cite navigation, a menu, a link, an unrelated excerpt, or an excerpt concerning a different bylaw.

Within the citation string, encode line breaks and quotation marks in the manner required for valid JSON. After decoding, the string must match the source passage exactly.

Final validity check

Before answering, silently confirm every point below.

The code is one of the 28 registry codes and is reproduced character for character, including its hyphens.

The verdict is exactly the registry partition of that code.

V2-PRECEDENT has the verdict non_pertinent and is selected only when the captured text names the individual process, identifies its case by a concrete element, and records the actual grant of that permission for the target act; otherwise the Gate 5 fallback applies. A process name or preliminary step is insufficient.

Every other non_pertinent classification satisfies all prerequisites of its exclusion, including closure when required. No missing information or uncertain inference supplies a prerequisite.

If the proposed exclusion fails that check, retain the applicable S code rather than searching for another exclusion based on the same missing evidence.

Every P code has the verdict pertinent.

Every S code has the verdict a_surveiller.

Every N code has the verdict non_pertinent.

The citation is direct, continuous, and no longer than 300 characters.

The ambiguous field is a JSON boolean and not a string.

The response is exactly one JSON object containing only the fields verdict, code, ambiguous, and citation, with no text before or after it.

Always return a valid JSON object, even when the supplied text is incomplete or interrupted. In that situation, use the applicable S fallback rather than omitting a field or adding explanatory prose.
