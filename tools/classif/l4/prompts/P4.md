Membership conditions for pertinent

A pertinent code requires all three substantive conditions. This is a membership test, not a ranking exercise.

1. Residential character: the regulated change concerns housing. It is not solely about commercial, industrial, institutional, agricultural, or recreational activity. An agricultural-land exclusion undertaken for housing counts as residential.
2. Opening direction: the change expands what is allowed rather than narrowing it.
3. Densification effect: the change can produce more dwellings than before.

A P code also requires general reach and demonstrated final adoption or entry into force. Missing evidence for any condition leads to an S code unless an exclusion is independently established. Missing evidence is never affirmative evidence for an exclusion.

Housing includes collective and shared housing. A residential change whose absence of dwelling-capacity effect is affirmatively established does not qualify.

An individual authorisation creates no general rule and never supports a P code. Examples are approval of a special project or an exemption for one applicant and one property.

Exclusion classes and fallback ordering

Class A consists of independently established exclusions: N-HORS-TERR, N-FAUX-POSITIF, N-DOUBLON, N-ODJ-SEUL, N-RETIRE, and V2-PRECEDENT.
- Each requires its own affirmative, citable evidence that matches the target act and covers it completely. Only then may it be decided immediately at its gate.
- Class membership never makes an exclusion decisive by itself.
- N-FAUX-POSITIF requires the identified non-planning operative subject defined at Gate 4, and is subject to the blocking conditions.
- V2-PRECEDENT requires the individual process, concrete case, actual grant, and coverage defined at Gate 5. A process name, the existence of a file, or the occurrence of a procedural step is not that form. Missing evidence of the case or the grant is never an irrelevant gap.

Class B consists of substantive exclusions: N-NON-RES, N-RESTRICTIF, N-FORME, N-UNIFAM, N-ADMIN, and N-ACCESSOIRE. Each requires the absence of every blocking condition and all three closure conditions:

1. The wording stating the concrete subject of the change is fully readable, not interrupted within a phrase.
2. That wording accounts for the entire stated subject. No additional clause, open-ended list, miscellaneous provision, or partly unreadable component of a combined amendment remains outside the exclusion.
3. The stated substance affirmatively establishes the exclusion.

A complete sentence containing only an instrument name or a generic amendment formula does not satisfy these conditions. Grammatical completeness is not substantive completeness.

When a relevant fact cannot be established, record the applicable S fallback and keep examining the item for independently supported exclusions. Do not stop at the first gap.
- A later Class A exclusion displaces that fallback only when every prerequisite is established.
- A later Class B exclusion displaces an ordinary substantive fallback only when closure is established and no blocking condition applies.
- Otherwise the first fallback recorded is the code.

Some fallbacks are recorded because the concrete subject of the change is unidentified, generic, or interrupted. Neither N-FAUX-POSITIF nor any Class B exclusion can displace such a fallback, because each requires that same subject.

Exception: the S-PORTEE-FLOUE fallback recorded at Gate 5 for an unresolved individual process yields only to a Class A exclusion fully established at Gates 1 to 4. No Class B exclusion, S-PLANIFIE, or P code displaces it.

Once a gate establishes a decisive code, do not reopen it merely because an element irrelevant to that code is unreadable.

Decision gates

Apply the gates in order, preserving the rules for items covering several acts.

Gate 1: captured documents without a single named target act

This gate applies only when no single target act is named. When the heading, summary line, description, or properties name a target act, other acts recorded in the capture never establish N-FAUX-POSITIF for that item. Judge the named act at Gate 4.

First, distinguish captured acts from document titles and navigation.

If only document or meeting titles, navigation, menus, or links to uncaptured documents remain:
- assign S-PORTEE-FLOUE with ambiguous true;
- cite the document or meeting title;
- never classify from navigation topics.

The absence of captured planning content in a title-only capture does not establish N-FAUX-POSITIF.

If acts are actually captured, examine their operative subjects. A captured act may concern urban planning when its subject is any of these:
- unreadable, generic, or interrupted;
- given only by a number;
- given only by the title of an amended bylaw.

Assign N-FAUX-POSITIF to the document only when both of these hold:
- every captured act is affirmatively identified as outside urban planning under Gate 4;
- no captured act has an unresolved subject that may concern planning.

Do not infer a non-planning subject from failure to recognize a planning act.

Judge captured content only. Do not speculate about uncaptured pages. Do not keep the document visible merely because uncaptured pages might contain other acts.

If at least one captured act is or may be an urban-planning act, continue through the remaining gates for that act. When several acts exist, choose one that remains visible. If none remains visible, choose the first.

In an agenda, each listed act is an agenda point.
- An agenda whose captured points exclusively concern established non-planning acts is N-FAUX-POSITIF.
- An agenda containing a point that is or may be a planning act proceeds to Gate 3.
- An agenda title without captured points is a title-only capture.

Gate 2: territory, duplication, and withdrawal

N-HORS-TERR applies only when the item or its metadata expressly places the municipality outside the monitored territories. Never infer this from the municipality's name.

N-DOUBLON applies only when the same act at the same procedural step is shown to be repeated, in one of two forms.

- Form 1: an earlier supplied item or a repetition within the capture.
  - Identity requires the same normalized target-act number together with the same title, subject, step, or sitting date.
  - Minor differences in accents, wording, or title completeness do not defeat established identity.
  - Different source pages, documents, or formats can capture the same act and step.
  - Without an act number, require the same specific title or subject at the same step and date. A generic parent-bylaw title is insufficient.
- Form 2: an express statement in item text or metadata that this is a repetition, duplicate, republication, renewed posting, or already captured signal for the same act and step.

A shared parent-bylaw number is insufficient. A later procedural step is a new act to classify on its own merits.

N-RETIRE applies only when the target item was withdrawn. A report, postponement, or adjournment is not withdrawal.

Gate 3: agenda-only evidence

Assign N-ODJ-SEUL only when both requirements are established:

1. The captured item itself is an agenda or an entry taken from an agenda.
2. No outcome is recorded for the target act in the capture.

Affirmative agenda evidence takes one of these forms:
- The document, page, heading, first title line, file name, document-type property, or source property identifies an agenda, draft agenda, meeting summons, or points for a forthcoming sitting.
- The text expressly places, adds, or lists the target point for consideration at a sitting.
- In a document not identified as minutes, the capture is a numbered list of sitting points that includes ordinary formalities and records no outcome for any point. Ordinary formalities include opening the sitting, adopting the agenda, a public question period, and closing the sitting.
- In a document with no single named target act, the act appears solely as a numbered point or heading in the list of points for a sitting, without an operative sentence for that act.

The following are not agenda evidence:
- a link, menu entry, or title naming an uncaptured agenda;
- a combined label covering agendas and minutes;
- a heading or numbering outside the forms above;
- the mere absence of voting language.

Within an identified agenda, a point remains a point for future treatment even when its heading names notice of motion, deposit, consultation, adoption, approval, refusal, or an individual-authorisation process. An agenda listing is not a council decision. Adoption of the agenda itself decides none of the items it lists. Points recited in a resolution adopting the agenda remain agenda points. An agenda-only entry is N-ODJ-SEUL, not V2-PRECEDENT.

A recorded outcome for the target act ends this gate. Continue through the later gates. Qualifying wording includes:
- a notice of motion actually given;
- a draft actually deposited;
- a motion moved, supported, and resolved;
- an act or draft adopted;
- a resolution recording a decision;
- a public notice reporting adoption of the target act or announcing consultation on it.

Such operative wording establishes occurrence even when the source type is unnamed. The following do not record an outcome:
- wording only announcing future adoption;
- empty fields for a mover, supporter, resolution number, or certification;
- a procedural-step, status, or category property;
- the title given to the signal.

A public notice is not an agenda.

When the source type is unclear and no outcome is recorded, do not infer N-ODJ-SEUL. Continue and treat the procedural step as unstated.

A recorded preliminary act may end this gate without proving a grant of individual permission. Evaluate the grant separately at Gate 5.

Gate 4: operative subject outside urban planning

N-FAUX-POSITIF requires an affirmatively identified non-planning operative subject and no change to a planning standard within the target act. All of the following must hold:
- The non-planning subject is stated by the target act's own title or operative wording. It is not stated by an excerpt concerning another act, an introductory recital, navigation, a link, or another act recorded in the same capture.
- That statement is readable through its end.
- The complete stated subject falls within matters outside urban planning. No added clause adopts, amends, or applies a planning instrument or standard.

Some bylaws or drafts have no identified operative subject and may concern urban planning. This is the case when the act is identified only by:
- its number;
- the word for a bylaw;
- a generic title such as a general or municipal bylaw with no stated matter;
- the title of the bylaw it amends;
- a title interrupted before its subject.

Read the operative action together with its object.
- A background reference to a building, construction, permit, street, zone, development project, or bylaw does not itself establish planning substance.
- The absence of a readable planning subject does not establish non-planning substance.
- Background references to financing, contracts, fees, safety, or other non-planning matters do not turn a planning act into a non-planning act.

Urban-planning acts include:
- Regional planning schemes and interim-control measures.
- Urban plans and special planning programmes.
- Zoning, subdivision, construction, and permits-and-certificates bylaws.
- Site-planning and architectural-integration controls, comprehensive development plans, minor exemptions, conditional uses, special projects, and demolition controls.
- Instruments creating an advisory planning committee, and mandates or recommendations assigned to that committee.
- Municipal works agreements and infrastructure contributions.
- Matters before the agricultural-land authority.
- Municipal rights of first refusal and land reserves.
- Mandates given to urban-planning professionals.

Acts outside urban planning include:
- Residual-materials management.
- Contract administration, tendering, and contract awards, except qualifying urban-planner mandates and municipal works agreements.
- Borrowing bylaws and expenditure decrees.
- Subsidies and financial-assistance programmes.
- Taxation, tariffs, fees, budgets, accounts, and financial reports.
- Nuisance controls.
- Traffic and parking on streets.
- Animal controls.
- Public and fire safety.
- Ethics, personnel, elections, and meeting formalities.
- Appointment, renewal, or remuneration of committee members, inspectors, and designated officers.
- Reports or statistics about permits already issued.
- Naming streets, parks, or places, and assigning civic numbers.

Apply these categories only when the target subject is actually established. A word associated with a category does not establish the category.

Contracts, mandates, and service awards:
- One is outside urban planning only when the captured wording states a deliverable or object outside urban planning.
- When its deliverable is unstated or interrupted, it is not affirmatively outside urban planning.
- When its deliverable may concern planning instruments, the development of a sector, housing, or a planning study, it is not affirmatively outside urban planning.
- In those cases, retain the fallback unless Gate 6 establishes S-MANDAT-AMONT.

Financing instruments: an explicit borrowing, expenditure, subsidy, tax, or tariff instrument remains financing and is N-FAUX-POSITIF. This holds even when it funds development, infrastructure, land acquisition, a land reserve, or a first-refusal measure. Financing such a measure is not creating it.

Municipal works agreements and infrastructure-contribution acts:
- Mentioning an agreement, money, financing, or a developer does not make such an act ordinary contract administration.
- Those references establish neither borrowing nor a non-planning subject.
- When the act's actual use, reach, or effect is unreadable, retain the applicable S fallback.

Ordinary property transactions: an ordinary purchase, sale, lease, easement, or taking of property for roads, parks, utilities, or municipal buildings is N-FAUX-POSITIF. It is not an exercise of a first-refusal right. This applies only when that ordinary transaction is the established operative subject. Do not infer that characterization from a generic land or agreement reference.

Exemptions under non-planning powers:
- An exemption, permit, or authorisation granted under a non-planning power is N-FAUX-POSITIF rather than V2-PRECEDENT, even for one applicant or address. Non-planning powers include nuisance, noise, traffic, animal, event, and public-land-occupation rules. This applies only when the captured wording names that power or matter.
- An exemption, permit, request, or authorisation is not affirmatively outside urban planning when its legal basis is unstated. The same holds when it concerns construction, alteration, a lot, a use, a building, or demolition. Examine it under Gate 5 and the later gates.

Off-street parking requirements connected with buildings are planning standards, not street-traffic regulation.

Mandatory guard:
- Sometimes the adopted or amended instrument is a planning instrument but its concrete subject is unreadable, generic, or interrupted. Planning instruments include zoning, subdivision, construction, an urban plan, permits and certificates, architectural integration, demolition, minor exemptions, conditional uses, special projects, and an advisory planning committee.
- In that case N-FAUX-POSITIF is unavailable. Preserve the S fallback.
- Do not infer N-FORME as a substitute. A non-planning instrument or decision is N-FAUX-POSITIF. A planning instrument without capacity effect can be N-FORME only after the separate proof required at Gate 9.

Gate 5: documented individual authorisation

First apply the instrument test. The following acts are general:
- a bylaw or draft bylaw;
- a notice of motion for a bylaw;
- a deposit of a draft bylaw;
- another act in the bylaw process.

Such an act remains general when one applicant requested it or a developer or project prompted it. It remains general when it names a project, address, or lot. It also remains general when it establishes or amends the process for a special project, minor exemption, conditional use, architectural-plan approval, or demolition.

Never assign V2-PRECEDENT to such a general instrument. Continue through the later gates. A named applicant, address, lot, or project alone does not show that an act is individual rather than a bylaw amendment.

A general process bylaw is not automatically N-FORME. Its concrete subject must satisfy Gate 9.

A general conditional-use or special-project bylaw can authorise uses, dwellings, densities, or heights that zoning does not otherwise allow. It is never N-FORME unless two things hold:
- the statement of what it changes is confined to application or processing procedure;
- it names no use, zone, density, height, or dwelling element.

A special planning programme (programme particulier d'urbanisme) is a general planning instrument, not a special project concerning one property.

A draft resolution for a special-project file is a step in an individual file, not a draft bylaw. It is not a documented grant merely because the process is individual.

S-PPCMOI-SERIE: assign it when the item or the supplied batch establishes several approved residential special-project cases converging on the same zone or zones under the same bylaw. Do not treat the matter as one individual case.

Otherwise, V2-PRECEDENT requires all four conditions, each established by matching evidence for the target act.

1. Individual process. The captured text names one of the following processes, in full or by its usual abbreviation, as the process the target act applies or as the subject of the item:
   - a special project concerning construction, alteration, or occupation of a property (projet particulier, PPCMOI);
   - a minor exemption (dérogation mineure);
   - a conditional use (usage conditionnel);
   - approval of plans under site-planning and architectural-integration controls (PIIA);
   - a demolition authorisation.
   A process mentioned only as background, or as the legal authority for a general instrument, is insufficient.
2. Identified case. A concrete element identifies the property, building, applicant, project, or file receiving permission.
   - Concrete elements are a civic address, a lot or cadastral number, an applicant or owner name, a building or project name, or a request or file number assigned to the case.
   - The operative permission must apply to that case.
   - The following do not identify the case: a municipality, zone code, sector, or category of properties; a process name; the word request or application; a resolution, sitting, or agenda-point number; the source address.
3. Actual grant. Matching captured wording records that the competent authority granted or approved the individual permission.
   - The approved object must be the permission itself. Acceptance of a request for processing, adoption of a draft, approval of a recommendation, and authorisation to hold a consultation are different objects.
   - A complete operative grant is sufficient even when accompanied by conditions.
   - A public notice can establish a grant when it expressly reports the completed approval of that same permission.
4. Coverage. The grant is the target act and covers its stated subject.
   - A background approval, an approval serving as legal basis for another act, or one approved component does not exclude a broader unresolved item.
   - If several cases are captured, every case must meet the requirements. An entry interrupted by the end of the capture does not.

When the first two conditions are shown, the act is a step in an individual file. Only a step recording the grant qualifies.

None of the following establishes a grant:
- the process name or acronym;
- a request or application;
- an advisory committee recommendation;
- council support or opinion;
- a consultation notice;
- a draft or later draft resolution, or adoption of a draft resolution;
- an intention to approve;
- a referral;
- a status or category label;
- a refusal or a postponement.

Do not infer eventual permission from the normal sequence of a process. Never infer individual reach or a grant merely because a process is commonly used for individual cases.

Gate 5 fallback: when an individual process is named but the case, the actual grant, the coverage, or the individual-versus-general distinction remains unresolved, assign S-PORTEE-FLOUE with ambiguous true. The only exception is a Class A exclusion already fully established at Gates 1 to 4.

This fallback applies at any procedural step to:
- headings or titles naming the process;
- requests, recommendations, notices, consultations, draft resolutions, resolutions, refusals, or decisions that do not establish all required facts;
- objects stated only as a zone, sector, or category of properties;
- descriptions of the process or its procedure that record no case;
- captures ending before identification or grant.

Do not substitute N-FORME because an unresolved file concerns architecture, plans, an approval procedure, or preliminary steps. Do not substitute N-RESTRICTIF because an individual request was refused. Do not replace this fallback with S-PLANIFIE or a P code.

One item, notice, or resolution may address both a step in an individual file and another planning act that is not an individual file, such as a bylaw or draft bylaw. Classify it by the act that remains visible. Assign V2-PRECEDENT only when no other planning act it covers would remain visible.

The following are not V2-PRECEDENT. Continue through the gates:
- A request to amend a bylaw or change zoning, even by one applicant for one lot.
- Council support for, or an opinion on, an application to the agricultural-land authority.
- Approval of a subdivision plan, comprehensive development plan, or integrated project, unless expressly granted under one of the listed individual processes.
- A municipal works agreement or infrastructure contribution.
- Named lots, applicants, or projects without a listed individual process.

None of these is N-FORME merely because approval or agreement is involved. An approval or agreement applying to one development creates no general rule and never supports a P code. Continue through the substantive checks and retain an S fallback when no exclusion is established.

A referendum-related step takes the character of the act it concerns. Examples are a request to participate in a referendum, a register, or referendum approval. Such a step is general for a bylaw or draft bylaw. For an individual resolution it does not itself prove the grant, and the four conditions still apply.

For an act naming one of the listed processes, uncertainty about whether it is a step in one file or a general rule means the identified case is not shown. Apply the Gate 5 fallback.

For any other act whose individual or general reach remains unclear, treat it as potentially general, continue, and set ambiguous true.

When all four conditions are established, V2-PRECEDENT is decisive regardless of use, direction, dwelling numbers, or architectural content. It takes precedence over Gates 6 to 10, subject to S-PPCMOI-SERIE and to the rule for items also covering another visible act.

Gate 6: special visible signals

Apply these codes only when the special act is itself the principal operative subject, not introductory material, background, or an incidental reference.

S-PREEMPTION: the act does one of the following:
- creates, records, or exercises a municipal right of first refusal over land;
- issues a notice associated with that right;
- creates a municipal land reserve.

This applies at any recorded procedural step, including a notice of motion or a draft of the instrument creating the right. An incidental reference or a financing instrument is insufficient.

S-MANDAT-AMONT: before a bylaw exists, a decision expressly retains an outside urban-planning professional to prepare development or revise planning instruments, and the deliverable is established. Once a bylaw has been introduced or drafted, apply the ordinary fallback and procedural-step rules to that bylaw.

Advisory planning committee (comité consultatif d'urbanisme):
- A routine study, review, or recommendation role is not S-MANDAT-AMONT.
- N-FORME applies to a committee mandate only when its complete actual assignment is expressly confined to a procedural advisory matter without capacity effect. The committee's name or usual role does not establish the assignment.
- A recommendation or opinion the committee issues on a particular request, file, project, property, or bylaw is not such a mandate. It is never N-FORME on account of the committee's role. Examine it under Gate 5 when it concerns an individual process, and otherwise on its own subject.
- A committee recommendation is not a grant.
- A recommendation concerning development does not show that the development lacks capacity effect.
- If the assignment cannot be read, retain the S fallback.

For an established first-refusal, reserve, or qualifying planner-mandate act, skip the direction and capacity gates. Apply N-NON-RES only if the complete special act is expressly exclusively non-residential. These special codes prevail over the procedural-step codes of Gate 10.

Gate 7: clerical and companion amendments

N-ADMIN applies only when the complete change is affirmatively confined to renumbering, correction of references or wording, or clerical alignment, with Class B closure. Alignment with a higher planning scheme is not clerical merely because alignment language appears. Assess it on its substance.

N-ACCESSOIRE applies only when a subdivision, construction, or technical-alignment amendment expressly implements an identified principal zoning change, with Class B closure. The principal change must be identified in the item or through the permitted batch relationship. Otherwise, classify the amendment on its own merits. Such a companion amendment is N-ACCESSOIRE rather than N-ADMIN.

Gate 8: residential character

Judge the resulting regulated use, not the name or origin of the zone. Housing added in a commercial zone is residential.

N-NON-RES applies only when the complete stated subject regulates exclusively commercial, industrial, institutional, public, agricultural, or recreational uses, and neither adds nor changes a dwelling use.
- This includes adding, authorising, or enlarging such a use, and constructing or extending such a building.
- Class B closure is required.
- A complete subject naming only such uses can establish this without the literal word "exclusively".
- The non-residential use must itself be the subject added, changed, or regulated. A zone label, zone code, or specification grid alone is insufficient.
- A change regulating only expressly non-residential uses or buildings is N-NON-RES even when the regulated matter, such as signs or outdoor storage, has no capacity effect.

Do not exclude a mixed residential and non-residential change.

Do not infer exclusively non-residential use from a zone label that sounds commercial when the subject could add housing. Do not infer it from unresolved references to chalets, seasonal accommodation, lodging, trailers, tourism potential, or housing for workers.

Collective and shared housing is residential. This includes residences for older persons, rooming houses, student housing, and social, community, supportive, or other collective housing. A mixed-use building or zone permitting dwellings is residential, including dwellings above ground-floor commercial premises.

An agricultural-zone exclusion or removal of agricultural designation undertaken for housing continues to the direction and capacity gates.

Do not infer housing solely from a zone code, specification grid, generic density reference, comprehensive revision, integrated-project reference, or agricultural-land reference.

If the affected use, concrete amendment subject, zone, territory, or reach cannot be identified, record S-PORTEE-FLOUE and continue. A later exclusion must satisfy its own proof, closure, and blocking-condition requirements.

Gate 9: direction and dwelling capacity

Evaluate direction through the effect on possible dwelling numbers.

An opening includes:
- adding or permitting dwellings or dwelling uses;
- creating a zone stated to be residential;
- increasing height, density, or dwelling numbers;
- reducing minimum lot area or width;
- relaxing setbacks, floor-area ratio, or parking requirements for principal buildings.

A narrowing includes:
- removing or prohibiting dwellings;
- lowering permitted density, height, or dwelling numbers;
- increasing minimum lot dimensions;
- imposing a moratorium;
- adding a constraint that reduces capacity.

Judge the object of a verb. Reducing a maximum height narrows capacity; reducing a minimum lot area expands it. A verb such as permit or add does not establish added dwellings when its object has no such effect.

N-RESTRICTIF applies only when every stated change to dwelling capacity narrows it and Class B closure is established over the complete subject. Evaluate a newly imposed narrowing constraint as N-RESTRICTIF. Evaluate an existing constraint on an opening as S-CONTRAINTE, below.

S-RESTRICTIF applies when the same item explicitly both expands and narrows dwelling capacity, each direction shown by an explicit capacity effect. Do not use it without two explicit and opposite effects. Mixed direction alone does not make the item ambiguous.

If the title states an opening but matching operative wording prohibits the same change, record S-INFO-MANQUANTE rather than excluding the item.

N-FORME applies only when all of these hold:
- all Class B closure conditions are met;
- no blocking condition applies;
- the complete concrete subject of what this act changes affirmatively has no possible dwelling-capacity effect.

The proof must concern what this act changes. It cannot consist of the type, number, or title of the parent instrument, the presence of approval vocabulary, or the absence of an express reference to dwellings.

There is no instrument-type shortcut to N-FORME.
- A generic amendment to permits and certificates, PIIA, demolition, minor exemptions, a committee bylaw, zoning, subdivision, construction, or an urban plan is insufficient.
- So is the adoption or replacement of such a bylaw under a title stating no other subject.
- Even when it is the only bylaw concerned, its category does not prove that the act is purely procedural or incapable of affecting dwelling capacity.

A fully specified change confined to non-capacity matters can qualify. These may include administrative approval procedures, architectural materials, fences, hedges, walls, sheds, garages, vehicle shelters, temporary shelters, pools, spas, signs, outdoor lighting, landscaping, mechanical equipment, outdoor storage, or garden structures. Each matter must be established as the whole change:
- without an unresolved dwelling, lot, height, density, use, or buildability component;
- with no standard governing lots, density, height, or dwelling use changing alongside it.

A change of use, a certificate authorising a use, or the occupation of a building by dwellings concerns use and is not N-FORME.

The following are insufficient for N-FORME:
- An amendment described only as modifying provisions, standards, criteria, or requirements, when their concrete content or capacity effect is unreadable.
- A complete non-capacity clause combined with miscellaneous provisions, other provisions, or an unreadable change.
- An amendment identified only by the kind, title, or number of the bylaw it amends. It is not closed: retain S-PORTEE-FLOUE.

A general bylaw establishing or amending an approval process qualifies only when both hold:
- the readable statement of what it changes is confined to procedure or other matters without capacity effect;
- it contains no use, lot, density, height, or dwelling element.

Citing an approval-process bylaw as the authority for an individual application establishes no general procedural amendment. Gate 5 governs that application.

Requirements for issuing construction permits can affect where dwellings may be built, even within a permits-and-certificates bylaw. Examples are lot conformity, access to a public or private street, and availability of water and sewer services. Do not treat them as merely procedural. Evaluate their direction and capacity effect. If either remains unreadable, preserve the S fallback.

Accessory buildings without dwellings may be non-capacity matters. Accessory, supplemental, garden-suite, or intergenerational dwellings add a unit and are assessed as capacity changes. This holds even when they are placed in an accessory building or listed as a complementary use. Do not infer that an unresolved accessory use excludes dwellings.

Gate 5 governs an individual-process file. Its missing case or grant cannot be converted into N-FORME.

If the affirmative no-capacity proof is missing, N-FORME is unavailable. Preserve the applicable S fallback.

N-UNIFAM applies only when the complete gain is confined to detached single-family homes in existing zones and Class B closure is established.

S-CONTRAINTE applies to a general opening limited by an existing constraint, such as required agricultural authorization, flood risk, missing services, or single-family-only use. General reach here means one of the following:
- the whole territory;
- a new zone;
- an enlarged perimeter or designation area;
- a collective agricultural-land decision.

S-INFO-MANQUANTE: record this fallback when the place and concrete subject are known and the use is residential, but whether the change expands or narrows capacity is unstated.

S-PORTEE-FLOUE: record this fallback in any of these cases:
- reach and direction are both unknown;
- the capacity effect cannot be determined;
- the use or concrete subject remains unidentified.

When uncertain between the two information fallbacks, use S-PORTEE-FLOUE.

Gate 10: visible-code ordering and procedural step

A fallback recorded at Gates 5, 8, or 9 prevails over every procedural-step rule. An early step does not resolve a missing use, subject, reach, direction, or capacity effect. Conflicting step evidence never replaces that fallback with S-PLANIFIE.

When no exclusion has been established, apply this ordering:

1. Preserve an established special code: S-PREEMPTION, S-MANDAT-AMONT, S-PPCMOI-SERIE, S-CONTRAINTE, or S-RESTRICTIF.
2. Otherwise, preserve the first unresolved substantive fallback. Gate 5 uncertainty retains S-PORTEE-FLOUE.
3. Use S-PLANIFIE only when all of these hold:
   - residential character, opening direction, and possible added dwelling capacity are established;
   - no fallback remains;
   - final adoption is absent, preliminary, unstated, or genuinely contradictory.
4. Use a P code only when those substantive conditions, general reach, and final adoption or entry into force are all established.
5. Otherwise, use S-PORTEE-FLOUE.

S-PLANIFIE includes an explicit phrase attached to the target act recording any of these:
- a notice of motion given;
- a first or later draft;
- deposit of a draft;
- consultation on a draft.

Adoption of a draft remains preliminary, including an initial or later draft.

Do not derive the target step solely from any of these:
- source type;
- a status label or a category;
- an agenda heading;
- future or announced adoption wording;
- an unrelated use of the word project or draft;
- the title of a parent instrument.

A draft individual resolution remains governed by Gate 5.

Judge the step from matching operative wording recording the target act. The following do not alter the current operative step and are not contradictory evidence about it:
- earlier notices, drafts, or consultations recounted in introductory material;
- labels for step, status, or category.

Adoption or entry into force of the numbered bylaw itself is final when the operative wording does not call the adopted instrument a draft. This holds even when the same capture records an earlier notice of motion or mentions earlier drafts. A status label alone never establishes finality.

When the substantive membership conditions are met but the step is unstated, use S-PLANIFIE with ambiguous true.

A contradiction requires incompatible evidence about the same target act and the same fact. Unrelated excerpts, historical recitals, and labels differing from operative wording are not contradictions. Unresolved competing operative passages require an S code. A stage contradiction produces S-PLANIFIE only when no earlier fallback applies.

Selecting a pertinent code

Only after every P-code requirement is established, select the first applicable code in this order:

1. P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE: named general instruments, such as alignment with a higher planning scheme, regional development objectives, a collective agricultural-land change, comprehensive revision, or repeal of a general standard.
2. P-PERIM-URB: enlargement of an urbanization perimeter or land-use designation area.
3. P-NOUV-ZONE: creation or opening of a new zone stated to be residential or to permit dwellings.
4. P-USAGE-MULTI: addition of multi-unit housing as an allowed use in an existing zone or zones.
5. P-TYPO-INTERM: opening of semi-detached, row, or contiguous housing forms.
6. P-PROJ-INTEGRE: introduction or relaxation of an integrated-projects framework.
7. P-DENSITE: increased density, height, or dwelling numbers, including accessory dwellings.
8. P-NORMES: relaxation of general setbacks, floor-area ratio, parking, lot-width, or lot-area standards.
9. P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE: another qualifying opening of general reach.

A new residential zone takes precedence over its accompanying density, height, use, and housing-form changes, including when it is formed from parts of existing zones. Adding dwellings or a use to an existing zone does not create a new zone.

For P-PERIM-URB and P-NOUV-ZONE, presume municipal initiative unless the item identifies another initiator.

The name of a general instrument never substitutes for the substantive membership conditions or finality.

Ambiguity

Set ambiguous to true for S-PORTEE-FLOUE and S-INFO-MANQUANTE. Also set it to true whenever the selected code depends on any of these:
- unresolved reach;
- an unidentified subject or case;
- an unestablished individual grant;
- an unresolved individual-versus-general distinction;
- absent direction;
- unknown capacity effect;
- an unstated or contradictory step;
- unclear source type;
- interrupted or materially truncated wording;
- another material judgment remaining after the rules.

Set ambiguous to false when explicit operative wording directly establishes the selected code and no material uncertainty remains. This includes a complete exclusion or a fully established P code.

An explicit mixed change coded S-RESTRICTIF is not ambiguous merely because it has both directions.

Setting ambiguous to true never makes an unsupported non_pertinent classification acceptable. Uncertainty about an exclusion requires an S code.

Citation

Select one continuous direct excerpt, no longer than 300 characters, from eligible evidence for the target item. Choose the passage that caused the selected code to apply.

Passage to cite, by code:
- Any non_pertinent code: the passage establishing the exclusion.
- V2-PRECEDENT: wording recording the actual grant, not merely the process, request, recommendation, or consultation. Prefer a grant passage that also contains the process name and the case identifier when they fit in one continuous excerpt.
- N-DOUBLON: the repetition indicator or the repeated heading.
- N-ODJ-SEUL: the agenda heading or point.
- Whole-document N-FAUX-POSITIF: the first line giving the document its title, after establishing the exclusion from its captured acts.
- P code: the opening and capacity wording.
- S-MANDAT-AMONT: the qualifying mandate.
- S-PLANIFIE: the procedural-step wording.

For a fallback code or S-PREEMPTION, select the most specific eligible title or subject phrase of the target act.
- Begin at the first word identifying it as a bylaw or draft. Continue through the end, or the captured cutoff, of what it amends, concerns, decides, or seeks to achieve.
- If no such identifying word exists, begin at its operative action. If there is none, begin at its subject phrase.
- A generic parent-bylaw title or an adoption formula never displaces a more informative amendment subject stated elsewhere.
- Introductory recitals are not titles.
- If no title or subject phrase exists, use the shortest clause giving the target act's number and step, breaking ties by first occurrence.

When no act can be identified, cite the first line titling the document, sitting, or record of proceedings.

After selecting the decisive wording, choose where to take it from in this order:
1. its earliest matching excerpt, treating a document item's text as its excerpt;
2. the description;
3. the item heading or summary line.

Within eligible occurrences, prefer wording inside a sentence recording the act over a heading.

Copy spelling, accents, capitalization, punctuation, internal whitespace, and line breaks exactly. Do not paraphrase, translate, insert omission marks, complete interrupted words, or replace line breaks with spaces. Prefer a single-line passage when it preserves the decisive wording. If the selected passage exceeds 300 characters, choose the shortest continuous portion that preserves that wording.

Never cite navigation, menus, links, unrelated passages, or an excerpt concerning a mismatching target bylaw.

Encode quotation marks and line breaks as JSON requires. After JSON decoding, the citation must match the source passage exactly.

Final check before returning JSON

Confirm silently:
- The code is one of the 28 registry codes, reproduced character for character.
- The verdict is the code's exact partition: P codes are pertinent, S codes are a_surveiller, and N codes and V2-PRECEDENT are non_pertinent.
- Every non_pertinent code has affirmative, citable evidence for all its prerequisites and covers the target act. No missing information or uncertain inference supplies a prerequisite.
- No blocking condition applies to a selected N-FAUX-POSITIF or Class B code, and every Class B code satisfies closure.
- N-FAUX-POSITIF rests on a complete, readable non-planning subject stated by the target act itself, or, for a document item, by its captured acts. It never rests on a number, a generic or interrupted title, the title of an amended bylaw, or an unidentified planning subject.
- N-FORME rests on the complete readable statement of what this act changes and on the affirmative absence of capacity effect. It never rests on a parent-bylaw kind or title, approval vocabulary, or silence about dwellings. The item is not a step applying an individual-authorisation process.
- No interrupted, residual, or unresolved component has been discarded to make an exclusion appear complete.
- V2-PRECEDENT has a named individual process, a concretely identified case, an actual documented grant, and complete coverage. Otherwise the Gate 5 fallback applies, unless Gates 1 to 4 independently establish an exclusion.
- Every P code has residential character, an opening, possible additional dwellings, general reach, and demonstrated final adoption or entry into force.
- Any unproved exclusion has been replaced with the applicable S code, not with another exclusion inferred from the same gap.
- The citation is direct, continuous, eligible, and no longer than 300 characters.
- The response is exactly one JSON object with only verdict, code, ambiguous, and citation, and ambiguous is a JSON boolean.

Always return valid JSON, including for incomplete or interrupted input. When the required exclusion evidence is missing, preserve visibility through the applicable S fallback. Never omit a field or add prose.
