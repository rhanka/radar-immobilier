## Steve's criteria

Steve wants an item only when all three conditions are met. This is membership, not ranking.

1. Residential: the bylaw concerns housing. Not commercial, industrial, institutional, agricultural, except a CPTAQ exclusion for housing, or recreational use.
2. Loosening: the change opens rather than tightens. The title verb usually shows the direction: permettre, autoriser, agrandir, ajouter, augmenter or assouplir versus interdire, limiter, préserver or restreindre.
3. Densification: the result allows more units than before. A residential loosening that cannot change the number of possible units is not pertinent.

Cross-cutting exclusions:

- An agenda item is not a council decision.
- An individual authorisation is not a general rule. A PPCMOI or derogation granted to one applicant for one property creates rights for no one else.

Asymmetry reserve: when scope or direction cannot be determined, keep the item shown with an S code. Do not hide an item merely because a passage is missing. A mixed change that both opens and tightens dwelling capacity must also remain shown.

## Decision procedure

Apply the following steps in order, subject to the full-scan and complete-object rules above. When a step establishes a decisive code, use it, except that a noted fallback is carried forward as provided above.

### Step 1: Territory, nature, duplicate and clerical or companion acts

- Expressly outside the targeted territories: `N-HORS-TERR`.
- The main operative object is outside urban planning and no planning norm is changed: `N-FAUX-POSITIF`.
  - Urban-planning acts include the schéma d'aménagement and RCI; the plan d'urbanisme and PPU; zoning, lotissement, construction and permit-and-certificate bylaws; PIIA, PAE, minor derogations, conditional uses, PPCMOI and demolition; the CCU; municipal-works agreements and infrastructure contributions; CPTAQ matters; rights of preemption and municipal land reserves; and urban-planner mandates.
  - Acts outside urban planning, even when they mention buildings, permits, construction, streets, a zone, a development project or the word règlement, include residual-materials management; contract management, calls for tenders and contract awards other than an urban-planner mandate or a municipal-works agreement; borrowing bylaws and expenditure decrees; subsidy and financial-assistance programs; taxation, tariffs, fees, budgets, accounts and financial reports; nuisances; traffic and on-street parking; animals; public and fire safety; and ethics, personnel, elections and meeting formalities.
  - Judge an operative financing instrument as financing: a borrowing bylaw, expenditure decree, subsidy, tax or tariff is `N-FAUX-POSITIF` even when it pays for a development, infrastructure, land acquisition or a land reserve. Its financing is not a preemption act.
  - Do not assign `N-FAUX-POSITIF` to a municipal-works agreement or an infrastructure-contribution act merely because it mentions an agreement or financing. Such a title alone establishes neither contract management nor borrowing; if the act's use, scope or effect is unreadable, keep the S fallback. A planning act that merely mentions financing or an agreement but changes a planning norm is not `N-FAUX-POSITIF`.
  - A plain purchase, sale, lease, servitude or expropriation of a property for streets, parks, utilities or municipal buildings is neither preemption nor an urban-planning act: `N-FAUX-POSITIF`.
  - Off-street parking requirements for buildings are a zoning norm, not a traffic matter.
- For a document item with no single named target act:
  - If none of the acts actually captured is an urban-planning act, code `N-FAUX-POSITIF` and cite the first line that titles the document. Judge only the acts actually captured; the possibility that uncaptured pages hold other acts does not keep the document shown. A captured bylaw whose subject cannot be read counts as possibly urban planning.
  - If at least one captured act is, or may be, an urban-planning act, code the document by that act through the rest of the procedure. If there are several, code by one whose code would be shown; if none would be shown, code by the first.
  - If only document or meeting titles, navigation, menus or links to uncaptured documents were captured, code `S-PORTEE-FLOUE` with `"ambiguous": true`. Never classify from navigation topics.
- Same act and same stage as an earlier supplied item, or the same act and stage expressly duplicated in the capture: `N-DOUBLON`. Identity may be shown by the same normalized bylaw or act number together with the same title, object, stage or date, or by the item's own text or metadata expressly identifying it as a repeat of an already captured signal for the same act and stage. A shared parent-bylaw number alone is not a duplicate. A later stage of the same act is coded on its own merits.
- The complete change is only renumbering, reference or wording correction, or clerical concordance: `N-ADMIN`. A schéma concordance is not clerical merely because it is called concordance; evaluate it substantively unless the clerical character is shown.
- A lotissement, construction or technical-concordance amendment expressly implementing an identified principal zoning change: `N-ACCESSOIRE`. If the principal change is not identified, code the item on its own merits.

### Step 2: Individual authorisation

- A PPCMOI, dérogation mineure, usage conditionnel, PIIA approval of plans or demolition authorisation is individual when the procedure is applied to an identifiable case anchor such as one request, applicant, address, lot, building, project, site plan or dossier. Code `V2-PRECEDENT`. The procedure applied to a request or property establishes individual reach; no recorded adoption, grant, refusal or other disposition is required. Every stage counts: request, public notice or consultation, CCU recommendation, draft resolution, agenda point, adoption, grant, refusal or conditions. `V2-PRECEDENT` applies whatever the use, direction, number of units, architectural content or stage, and outranks Steps 3 to 8. A draft resolution for a PPCMOI is a stage of that individual file, not a draft bylaw.
- Several granted residential PPCMOI cases converging on the same zone or zones against the same bylaw, visible in the item or supplied batch: `S-PPCMOI-SERIE`, instead of `V2-PRECEDENT` for a single case.
- The following are not individual files, so continue the procedure:
  - an amendment to a bylaw, including a zoning, lotissement, plan d'urbanisme or PIIA bylaw, even when one applicant requested it or it affects named lots;
  - a bylaw that frames one of these procedures in general; judge its object at Steps 5 to 7, and a general procedural bylaw may be `N-FORME` under Step 7;
  - the council's support of, or opinion on, an application to the CPTAQ;
  - named lots or applicants without one of the procedures above.
- If it remains unclear whether the act is individual or general after these rules, treat it as general, continue the procedure, and set `"ambiguous": true`.

### Step 3: Withdrawal and agenda-only evidence

- The target item was withdrawn: `N-RETIRE`. A report, postponement or adjournment is not a withdrawal.
- Code `N-ODJ-SEUL` only when the item positively shows that its only evidence for the target act is an agenda and it records no outcome for that act. Agenda evidence requires one of these forms: the document, page, heading, file name or source property identifies an agenda, a draft agenda, a convocation or notice of convocation, or the points of an upcoming sitting; or the text states that the point is placed or added on the agenda. A heading or numbering outside an identified agenda is not agenda evidence, and the absence of voting words in unrelated text is not sufficient.
- Inside an identified agenda, an entry naming an act to be dealt with is a point to be dealt with, not a recorded act, even when the entry names a notice of motion, a draft deposit or an adoption. Adoption of the agenda itself is not a decision on the items listed in it.
- A recorded outcome for the same act ends this step, so continue the procedure. Recorded outcomes include operative wording stating that a notice of motion was given, a draft was deposited, a proposal was moved, seconded and resolved, an act or draft was adopted, or a resolution recording the decision; and a public notice that reports the adoption of the act or of a draft, or announces its consultation. Such operative wording establishes the act even when the source type is not named. Wording that only announces a future adoption does not establish it. Blank mover, seconder, resolution-number or certification fields do not by themselves establish it.
- A public notice is not an agenda.
- If the source type cannot be identified and no outcome is recorded, do not use `N-ODJ-SEUL`; continue, and the stage will be unstated at Step 8.

### Step 4: Special shown signals whose main object precedes the ordinary checks

Apply this step only when the special act is the main operative object, not a recital, background mention or incidental reference.

- Creation, registration or exercise of a droit de préemption, avis de préemption or municipal land reserve: `S-PREEMPTION`.
- A decision expressly commissioning an urbaniste-conseil to prepare a development or revise planning instruments: `S-MANDAT-AMONT`. Once a bylaw has been moved or drafted, use `S-PLANIFIE` instead.
- A routine CCU study, review or recommendation mandate is not `S-MANDAT-AMONT`. If its complete object is only that procedural advisory function, use `N-FORME`; if the actual assignment cannot be read, retain the applicable S fallback.
- For a genuine preemption or planner-mandate act, do not apply the direction or capacity steps. Apply `N-NON-RES` only if the item expressly makes the special act exclusively non-residential.

### Step 5: Residential use

Judge the resulting use regulated by the change, not merely the label or origin of the zone. Adding housing in a commercial zone is residential.

- Code `N-NON-RES` when the amendment's regulated resulting use is expressly only commercial, industrial, institutional or public, agricultural, or recreational. The non-residential use must be the object being added, changed or regulated; a zone name or zone code alone is insufficient.
- Do not require the words seulement or exclusivement when the complete stated amendment object names only non-residential uses and adds or changes no dwelling use.
- Do not code `N-NON-RES` from hedged or unresolved dwelling-related wording such as potentiel, chalet, hébergement, roulotte or housing for workers unless the resulting regulated use is established as exclusively non-residential.
- A CPTAQ exclusion or dézonage for housing continues to the direction and capacity steps.
- Do not infer housing from a zone code, specification grid, generic density reference, refonte, integrated-project reference or CPTAQ reference alone.
- If the affected use, concrete object or geographic scope cannot be identified after the exclusion steps above, note fallback `S-PORTEE-FLOUE` and continue checking direction, capacity and stage for a later complete exclusion.

### Step 6: Direction

Judge direction by the effect on dwelling capacity. The verbs are evidence of that effect: opening effects include permitting or adding dwellings or dwelling uses, creating a new zone stated to be residential, increasing height, density or dwellings, reducing a minimum lot area or width, or loosening setbacks, floor-area ratio or parking for principal buildings. Tightening effects include prohibiting or removing dwellings, reducing density, height or permitted dwellings, increasing minimum lot dimensions, imposing a moratorium, or adding a constraint that reduces dwelling capacity.

- Réduire depends on its object: reducing a maximum height tightens; reducing a minimum lot area opens.
- Every dwelling-capacity change tightens: `N-RESTRICTIF`.
- The same item both opens and tightens dwelling capacity: `S-RESTRICTIF`, not ambiguous merely because the direction is mixed.
- A bylaw that creates a tightening constraint is `N-RESTRICTIF`, not `S-CONTRAINTE`; a pre-existing constraint is handled at Step 7.
- Where and what are known, the use is residential, and only the direction is missing: fallback `S-INFO-MANQUANTE`.
- Where scope and direction are both unknown: fallback `S-PORTEE-FLOUE`.

### Step 7: Capacity to build

Ask whether the result can allow more units than before. Loosened lot, setback, floor-area-ratio or parking norms for principal buildings can allow more units.

- Code `N-FORME` when the change cannot alter the number of possible units, in either of these cases:
  - The complete stated object is an approval procedure or a matter with no capacity effect, and no lot, density, height or dwelling-use norm also changes. Such matters include permit and certificate procedures, demolition procedure, PIIA procedure or criteria, driveways, occupancy or maintenance, fences, sheds or remises, signs, garden structures, architecture and materials.
  - The only bylaw amended is by nature an approval-procedure or committee bylaw, and no change to zoning, lotissement, the plan d'urbanisme, uses, lots, density, height or dwellings is stated. These bylaws are permis et certificats, PIIA, démolition, comité consultatif d'urbanisme and dérogations mineures. This exclusion covers the act's complete object.
- Accessory buildings are `N-FORME`; accessory dwellings add a unit and are evaluated as capacity changes.
- A zoning, lotissement or plan d'urbanisme title shows no amendment object by itself. Distinguish the parent bylaw's title from the amendment's object, and keep the fallback when the object is unreadable.
- If the complete object cannot change possible units but was not already captured above, it remains `N-FORME` only when the complete stated object positively shows no capacity effect.
- A gain limited to detached single-family homes in existing zones: `N-UNIFAM`.
- A general-scope opening limited by a pre-existing constraint such as required CPTAQ authorisation, a flood zone, missing services or a single-family-only use: `S-CONTRAINTE`. General-scope openings are the whole territory, a new zone, an enlarged perimeter or affectation area, or a collective CPTAQ decision.
- If the effect on units cannot be told and no earlier decisive code applies: fallback `S-PORTEE-FLOUE`, unless the residential object is identified and only direction is missing, in which case the fallback is `S-INFO-MANQUANTE`.

### Step 8: Stage and pertinent codes

A fallback noted at Steps 5 to 7 wins over every stage rule in this step. Never replace that fallback with `S-PLANIFIE` because the stage is preliminary or because stage evidence conflicts.

- Avis de motion, premier projet, second projet, projet déposé, or consultation notice: `S-PLANIFIE`.
- An adopted draft, including adoption of a premier or second projet, remains `S-PLANIFIE`.
- A P code requires a shown final adoption or entry into force. Adoption or entry into force of the numbered bylaw itself is final when the operative text does not call the adopted instrument a projet. Do not infer final adoption from a status tag.
- If eligible in substance but the stage is not stated: `S-PLANIFIE` with `"ambiguous": true`.
- Preemption and planner-mandate codes from Step 4 win over the stage codes in this step.

When a P code is justified, choose it in this order:

1. Named general instruments such as schéma concordance, OGAT, a collective CPTAQ decision, refonte or repeal of a general norm: `P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE`.
2. Enlargement of an urbanization perimeter or affectation area: `P-PERIM-URB`.
3. Creation or opening of a new residential zone: `P-NOUV-ZONE`.
4. Addition of multi-unit housing in existing zones: `P-USAGE-MULTI`.
5. Semi-detached, row or contiguous housing: `P-TYPO-INTERM`.
6. Integrated projects: `P-PROJ-INTEGRE`.
7. Density, height or number of dwellings, including accessory dwellings: `P-DENSITE`.
8. Setbacks, floor-area ratio, parking, lot width or lot area: `P-NORMES`.
9. Any other general-scope opening: `P-VILLE-TERRITOIRE`.

Creation or addition of a new zone is `P-NOUV-ZONE`, not merely `P-DENSITE` or `P-USAGE-MULTI`. `P-PERIM-URB` and `P-NOUV-ZONE` presume City initiative unless the item names another initiator.

## Fallback calibration and tie-breaks

- `S-PORTEE-FLOUE`: where, what use, concrete amendment object or geographic scope is unknown, for example in principle a bare amendment to a zoning bylaw with no stated object, a capture containing only a meeting or document title, or a truncated title whose purpose cannot be read.
- `S-INFO-MANQUANTE`: where and what are known and residential, but the opening or tightening effect is not given. Use `S-INFO-MANQUANTE` only for that case; when scope and direction are both unknown, or there is any doubt between the two fallbacks, use `S-PORTEE-FLOUE`.
- `S-PLANIFIE`: the planning act and its preliminary stage are identified, even if later details remain to be confirmed.
- Do not replace a scope fallback with a non-pertinent code unless a complete positive exclusion has been established.
- Do not replace an S item with a P code unless residential character, loosening, added capacity and final stage are all shown.
- If only a document or meeting title remains after excluding navigation and unrelated content, use `S-PORTEE-FLOUE` and quote that title.
- `N-FAUX-POSITIF` is for an act outside urban planning, even if it concerns buildings or permits. `N-FORME` is for an urban-planning act with no capacity effect.
- An agenda whose points concern only acts outside urban planning is `N-FAUX-POSITIF`. An agenda point on an urban-planning act is `N-ODJ-SEUL` when Step 3 is met.
- A droit de préemption or réserve foncière is `S-PREEMPTION`; its financing instrument is `N-FAUX-POSITIF`.
- Use `N-ADMIN` only when the change is shown to be clerical. A companion amendment to an identified principal zoning change is `N-ACCESSOIRE`, not `N-ADMIN`.
- A contradiction is incompatible evidence about the same act and the same fact; unrelated excerpts do not count. If competing passages concern the same act and the same fact and cannot both be true, retain the item in a_surveiller. A title that opens while the operative body forbids the same change produces `S-INFO-MANQUANTE`. Contradictory stage evidence produces `S-PLANIFIE`, unless a fallback noted at Steps 5 to 7 applies.

## Ambiguity

Set `"ambiguous": true` when the selected code rests on `S-PORTEE-FLOUE` or `S-INFO-MANQUANTE`, an unresolved scope, missing direction, an unstated or contradictory stage, unclear source type, truncation, the unclear individual-versus-general treatment in Step 2, or a material judgment call that remains after the tie-breaks.

Set `"ambiguous": false` when explicit operative wording directly establishes the selected code, including a complete exclusion or a fully established P code.

A mixed opening-and-tightening item coded `S-RESTRICTIF` is not ambiguous solely because it is mixed.

Ambiguity never justifies a non_pertinent code.
